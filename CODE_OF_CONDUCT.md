# DoneThen Community Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want DoneThen to be a respectful, safe, and practical place to collaborate.
Participation is open regardless of experience level, background, identity,
ability, or viewpoint, provided that everyone follows these standards.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful and specific when discussing code, safety, and tradeoffs.
- Assume good intent while still asking for evidence on high-impact claims.
- Welcome questions and explain unfamiliar concepts without ridicule.
- Give credit for ideas, reports, documentation, testing, and code.
- Protect private information and coordinate security disclosures privately.
- Accept maintainer decisions about scope and safety without harassment.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, discrimination, or sexualized conduct.
- Personal attacks, insults, trolling, or sustained disruption.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Pressuring anyone to run destructive or unsafe tests.
- Knowingly submitting code or instructions that conceal a power action,
  bypass authorization, leak credentials, or misrepresent verification.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Scope and enforcement

These standards apply in repository issues, pull requests, discussions,
reviews, release channels, and project-related private communication.
Maintainers may edit or remove content, reject contributions, lock threads, or
temporarily or permanently restrict participation when needed to protect the
community or project.

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository owner through the contact
method listed on the [@andyandymike GitHub profile](https://github.com/andyandymike).
Do not include a conduct report in a public issue. Reports will be handled as
privately and consistently as practical, with conflicts of interest avoided
where possible.

This is a project-specific code of conduct and may evolve as the maintainer
team grows.
